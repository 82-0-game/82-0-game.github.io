Team and Era: Both Conditions Have to Match

Played for that team? That is only half the question. The 82-0 game also checks the period in the draw; a familiar name can match one condition and miss the other.

🧷

The team matches. The period does not.

A player's connection to a franchise can be correct while the draw still excludes that part of his career. The relevant stint must belong to the offered period as well. Searching the familiar name only searches the current list; it cannot bring in a card from another era.

In the exercise below, Birch belongs to Team A but Window 2. Selecting Team A with Window 1 passes the team test and fails the period test. One correct answer is not enough.

🔀

The period matches. The team does not.

Remembering a player as active in a period says nothing about which franchise's version you will receive. His stint elsewhere can fit the time window while failing the team's condition.

Cedar belongs to Team B and Window 1. With Team A and Window 1 selected, the window is right and the team is wrong. Changing only the window cannot fix that mismatch.

↔️One reel moves; the other stays

TEAM respin
Keep the period, change the franchise, then read the new pool.
ERA respin
Keep the franchise, change the period, then read the new pool.

That preserved condition gives a respin its purpose. If you want to keep the franchise connection, changing ERA addresses the other side. If you want to keep the period, changing TEAM addresses the franchise side. Neither action reserves a specific star.

The game supplies the candidate list for the pair; matching historical membership does not promise every eligible person is represented. Check the remaining options shown in your run. The TEAM decision guide and ERA decision guide discuss when to use them; the membership matrix explains what the change means.

🔎Missing, hidden or unusable?

  1. MembershipThe current TEAM/ERA pair defines the draw.
  2. Viewing restrictionsSearch and role controls can hide part of that draw.
  3. PlacementThe candidate's position labels and current action determine its usable targets.

These are three different explanations for “I can't pick the player I expected.” Clear a search to test the view. Read a card's roles to test placement. Changing a reel tackles membership, so it is a much larger action than clearing a filter.

The 82-0 game becomes easier to reason about when you identify the failed test first. Use the control map to locate the view, the board check for destinations, or a player profile when you need to untangle a career chapter.

Change one reel with a clear purpose, and read the resulting TEAM/ERA pair before looking for a favorite.
