Season Projection: Read the Result You Received

A strong summary and a strong record are related through the game’s conversion, not an equation printed on the cards. This 82-0 game guide shows what you can check after a finish.

Illustrative result76–676 wins · 6 losses

🏁Read a record as a record

This invented result has eighty-two games: seventy-six wins plus six losses. The bar below visualizes that count. It does not estimate what any particular lineup would produce.

The record is the output. Your roster's five category contributions are inputs to a different stage. Reading both stages keeps an impressive stat line from turning into an unsupported promise about wins.

The completed draft receives its result. The result presentation shows that returned record; its animation does not reveal the calculation that produced it.

The game's explanation describes a nonlinear projection, with stronger demands toward a perfect season. That gives useful direction, but not a calibrated curve. It cannot tell you that one extra RPG is worth a fixed number of wins, or locate an exact “perfect record” cutoff.

🔍Three separate checks

Record arithmetic
Wins plus losses should describe the stated season length. This checks the record's presentation, not the quality of its statistical model.
Contribution arithmetic
Compare like categories. Team totals can be formed before display rounding, so printed card fields may not reproduce the last decimal exactly.
Context identity
Keep the franchise-era entries attached to the run. A same-name card from a different context is not necessarily the same statistical input.

A flat tradeoff deserves a flat description: you gained one category and gave up another. The card lab makes the exchange visible without turning it into an invented win calculator.

📝Keep a run you can actually compare

Before placement, note the candidate's context and displayed fields if you want to study the draft later. Filled jerseys do not provide a permanent five-stat notebook. After completion, record the returned wins and losses beside those notes.

For a second run, state exactly what changed. If several cards, franchises or eras changed, the record difference cannot isolate the effect of one category. Even a carefully described pair of runs is an observation, not enough to reverse-engineer the complete model.

Use the precision explanation for a small manual-total discrepancy and the last-slot audit for the final decision before completion. In the 82-0 game, a clear record of the choice is more useful than a formula guessed from one finish.
