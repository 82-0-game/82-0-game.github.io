20-0 Football: Roles, Offers and the Perfect Run

Build twelve football roles across offense and defense. This playbook explains the available FLEX choices, the player season on the card, Classic or Gridiron IQ, and the full twenty-win objective.

🏈What is the football version of the 82-0 game?

20-0 takes the historical-player draft into American football. Its full objective is seventeen regular-season victories followed by three playoff victories. The name describes that twenty-win challenge, while the roster asks you to build both sides of the ball.

Both offensive FLEX places support RB, WR and TE; QB is a separate job. A supplied alternate position can also affect a card’s eligibility. Use those options to preserve a dedicated opening you still need, while checking the candidate’s offered roles.

Defense: next six

  1. EDGE
  2. DT
  3. LB
  4. CB
  5. S
  6. D-FLEX

D-FLEX supports EDGE, DT, LB, CB and S. Those are also the five dedicated defensive jobs. If a candidate can serve more than one supported role, compare the opening you spend with the remaining jobs on this side of the ball.

🎞️A famous name still has a specific season

Read the season on the card before judging the player. Modern spin windows cover five-year periods, while legend windows can span more. The offer leads to a season version; it should not be read as the NBA game's franchise-decade average.

A player's best-known campaign may be outside the version in front of you. Compare the offered candidates for the job that is open, using the information shown by the selected mode.

👁️Classic or Gridiron IQ?

Visibility changes what you can inspect before the pick
ModeAt selection timeUseful starting point
ClassicStats and ratings are shownCheck the season line alongside the role
Gridiron IQStats and ratings are hiddenUse team-and-period knowledge and acknowledge gaps in recall

Classic is the cleaner introduction if you recognize stars but cannot confidently place their best seasons. Gridiron IQ gives that memory work a purpose. Switching visibility changes how you make the selection; it does not change the football jobs that remain.

🎰

Give the side's reroll a reason

The standard draft has one discretionary reroll per side of the ball. Before spending the current side's allowance, identify the problem: which unfinished role is poorly served by the offer? After rerolling, judge the new candidates against that same need.

Keeping a serviceable offer also has a cost: it commits you to one of its candidates. Compare that cost with using the remaining reroll now. An empty or unusable offer can have a separate recovery flow. Check the on-screen availability as you move between units rather than counting every recovery as a spent discretionary reroll.

🛡️Finish the second half of the roster

When defense begins, treat it as six new role decisions. Look at EDGE, DT, LB, CB, S and the remaining flex opening together. A full run needs the complete twelve-player roster, so keep the unfinished defensive jobs in view until the last placement.
