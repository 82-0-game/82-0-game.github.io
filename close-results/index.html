The 82-0 Game After an 81-1 or 80-2 Finish

You were one or two losses short. Before another draft, inspect the decision that deserves a closer look: its legal alternatives, the exchange you accepted and the lineup you actually finished.

📍How close were 81–1 and 80–2?

80–280 wins · 2 losses · 82 games

Completed win share: 97.6% after rounding.

Both finishes are close in the score notation: one or two losses remain. The bars divide completed wins by eighty-two; they say nothing about the odds of your next attempt. An 81–1 roster is worth examining, but the readout supplies no numerical distance from an 82–0 threshold.

🔎Save the decision behind the close result

Before replaying, capture the actual finish and its five cards. Then pick the decision that felt hardest: the slot, the offered alternatives and the stat exchange you accepted. That is a useful starting point for a review.

The final pick is memorable because it comes last. Check whether it was genuinely the unresolved choice. An earlier placement may have spent a flexible slot or left the final offer with fewer legal destinations. Follow the last-pick walkthrough when placement options are the question.

Review case · hypothetical decision, not a played run

🧩“More assists” is only half a comparison

Imagine that your rejected legal alternative offered more APG but less RPG. A useful note includes both changes and the slot you would use. The selected card's rebound contribution remains part of the exchange, even when the missing win tempts you to focus on assists.

A note you cannot test

“The team needed one more assist.”

This names a category but leaves out the candidate, its cost and its available destination.

A note worth revisiting

“At this open slot, the alternative raised APG and reduced RPG; both candidates were eligible.”

This identifies a real kind of decision. Add the actual card versions and printed differences from your offer.

The card comparison guide handles mixed rows. Use it to make the exchange explicit, then keep your interpretation separate from the returned season total.

🔁Carry one question into the next draft

  1. Choose the question.Example: did you spend a flexible position earlier than necessary?
  2. Inspect the new opportunity.Compare the new pool and destinations; the previous rejected card may not be offered again.
  3. Retain both outcomes.Different draws and rosters can change together, so record the new attempt even if it finishes lower.

A single close finish does not isolate a cause. Several well-described attempts can still improve how you choose: which slots you reserve, which statistical exchanges you notice and which draws you decide to keep.

Keep the target exact

In the 82-0 game, eighty-one wins remain one short of the perfect condition. Use the success reference to judge a finish. Use this review to decide what question you want the next attempt to answer.
