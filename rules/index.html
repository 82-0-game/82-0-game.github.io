82-0 Game Rules: What Makes a Pick Valid?

A name in the candidate list is only part of a valid pick. These NBA 82-0 game rules explain the drawn pool, legal positions, roster moves and the separate TEAM and ERA controls.

📋The ordinary draft, in six checks

🎰

The roll sets the pool

A franchise and an era determine which versions are offered. Searching a name does not substitute a different draw.

🖐️

Five rounds fill the roster

Each completed placement supplies one player. The returned season record follows the finished five-player board.

👕

A position must be available

The selected card needs a compatible open slot. A filled jersey cannot accept an ordinary second placement.

🔀

TEAM is its own action

A team respin changes the franchise while retaining the era. Its availability is shown on its own control.

⏳

ERA is a different action

An era respin changes the time window while retaining the franchise. An unused ERA allowance is not permission to press TEAM.

🧮

The result uses player output

Points, rebounds, assists, steals and blocks contribute to the projection. Familiarity or fame is not an extra visible stat column.

🧩What makes a candidate usable?

In the NBA 82-0 game, appearing in the pool and fitting the board are separate requirements. A candidate can match the roll but have no available position left. That is a roster constraint, not evidence that the player belongs to another franchise.

Check the positions on the selected card, then the free places on the board. G and F are broad list filters: a visible guard does not necessarily fit both PG and SG. The individual jersey highlights settle where that card can go.

📍Roster labels and placement

PG, SG, SF, PF and C are the five roster positions. Each holds one player. Cards with several compatible positions give you a choice of placement; the board also permits a move or swap when every affected player fits the destination. A selected candidate must be cleared before rearranging placed jerseys.

🎟️Read the allowance on this run

Read TEAM and ERA separately. Each control shows its own remaining availability and any condition attached to it. Using one does not transfer an allowance from the other. Once an action completes, use the updated display for the next decision.

The new draw replaces the candidate opportunity you were inspecting. You keep the already placed roster, but you do not reserve a player from the discarded pool. Before pressing a control, identify which usable card you would otherwise select.

What stays fixed?

TEAM preserves the era. ERA preserves the franchise. Identify the part worth retaining before deciding which control suits the problem.

What gets used?

The action uses its own displayed allowance or condition. Check the updated control after the action rather than assuming a previous count still applies.

📐Card data and the 82-game result

Classic displays PPG, RPG, APG, SPG and BPG. HoopIQ withholds those numbers during selection. The modes change the information you can use, so keep the mode attached to any record you compare.

A card describes production for the offered franchise-and-era entry. It is not automatically the player’s career average or best individual season. Older defensive columns may include estimates where historical records are incomplete; the displayed card remains the input used by the game.

The season projection is nonlinear. Five column totals can help explain your roster, but a sum of raw points, rebounds and assists is not a published formula for its wins. Neither an individual card nor a conventional lineup arrangement proves an 82-0 finish before the game returns it.
